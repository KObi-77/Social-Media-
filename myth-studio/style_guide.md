# Channel Style Guide — "Untold Origins"

Every agent in the pipeline reads this file. Edit it to change the channel's voice;
no code changes are needed.

## Channel promise
The real, original, often darker or stranger versions of myths, folklore, fairy tales
and history — told so they are funny, surprising and true.
Traditions in rotation: African (Yoruba, Igbo, Akan/Ashanti, Zulu, Egyptian and more),
Norse, Japanese, Greek, and the original grim versions of European fairy tales.

## Narrator voice
- Deadpan, dry and a little sarcastic, like a friend who has read too many old books.
- Talks to the viewer directly ("you", "we").
- Treats absurd events with total calm. The calmer the narrator, the funnier the story.
- Never mocks the cultures the stories come from. The joke is the situation, the
  characters' decisions, and the gap between the myth and modern life. It is never
  the people who told these stories.

## Short format (60–90 seconds, ~150–220 spoken words)
| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Hook | One sentence. A strange claim or question. No "Hi guys", no intro. |
| 3–20s | Setup | Who, what they want, what went wrong. |
| 20–50s | Escalation | Each beat is more absurd than the last. A joke every 5–8 seconds. |
| 50–70s | Twist | A true, surprising reveal from the original source. |
| Last 3s | Loop | The final line leads back into the first line so the video replays. |

## Long format (8–12 minutes, ~1,200–1,800 spoken words)
Same voice. Structure: cold-open hook (15s) → why this story matters → 3–4 acts, each
ending on a mini-cliffhanger → the big twist → "what the original actually says vs
the version you know" → call-back joke → teaser for a related story.

## Humor toolkit (use at least three per short)
- Deadpan description of something insane.
- Modern framing: HR complaints, group chats, customer reviews, LinkedIn posts, therapy.
- Rule of three: two normal items, then a ridiculous third.
- On-screen text that contradicts or comments on the narrator.
- Callback to an earlier joke.
- Understatement ("It did not go well for the giants.").

## Twist types
- Reversal: the "villain" was right, or the hero is the problem.
- "It's real": the absurd detail really is in the original source.
- Hidden cause: a tiny detail caused the big event.
- Original version: the Disney/modern version left out the important part.
- Irony: the plan worked too well.

## Hard rules
1. **The twist and every key claim must be true to a named source** (a specific
   myth text, collection or historical record). If sources disagree, say "in one
   version...". Never invent "facts".
2. Never copy wording, characters or art from another creator or a modern copyrighted
   adaptation. Retell from the original public-domain source in our own words.
3. Gore and darkness are fine when hinted at; describe nothing graphic. Must pass
   YouTube's advertiser-friendly guidelines.
4. No hook may start with "Did you know".
